# Check: three minutes

Run at handoff (stage 5). Name what the person gets at the third second, the thirtieth, and the third minute.

## Why

A poster gives everything in a second and then goes silent. A place keeps giving.

The whole difference between a good page and a thing people return to lies in the third minute: in Bruno Simon's portfolio, around the tenth minute you find bowling pins nobody mentioned — and that is why people remember it for years.

## The rule

Write three lines:

    3 seconds    what is visible without doing anything — and the main visitor
                 already knows whose this is and what is here
    30 seconds   what opened once the person started fiddling
    3 minutes    what the one who stayed found

Each line contains a concrete object or event. "More detail", "deeper immersion", "unfolds gradually" don't count: those are promises, not discoveries.

## Hard conditions

- At the third minute there must be something **new**, not "the same, but denser". Turning up a parameter is not a discovery.
- The third-minute discovery is **labelled nowhere**. No arrows, no hints, no "try hovering". A labelled discovery stops being one.
- The discovery is **not required**: whoever left at thirty seconds must not feel cheated.
- If there is nothing for the third line, the work is not ready. The cure is not polish but one new thing inside.

## What does not count as a third minute

- Settings, theme toggles, sliders
- Another section further down the page
- Text that could have been read at the third second
- A counter going up

## Gate

- [ ] Three lines are written, each with an object or event
- [ ] At the third minute there is something that wasn't there at thirty seconds
- [ ] The discovery is labelled nowhere and is not required
- [ ] The lines are recorded in `LINES.md`
